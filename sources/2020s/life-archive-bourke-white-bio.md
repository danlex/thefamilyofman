## Relevance

Tier-3 institutional photographer-archive page at LIFE for Margaret Bourke-White (1904–1971), one of the original four staff photographers hired by Henry Luce in 1936; her *Fort Peck Dam* photograph was reproduced on the first cover of LIFE (November 23, 1936). Bourke-White has six plates in *The Family of Man* (count by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-06).

## Key excerpts / pages

Verbatim quotations from the page fetched 2026-05-06:

- Birth and death dates "1904–1971".
- "In 1929, she created the lead story for Fortune magazine's first issue."
- "In 1930, became the first Western photographer permitted into the Soviet Union."
- "In 1936, collaborated with Erskine Caldwell (her future husband) on a documentary book about Southern rural poverty titled *You Have Seen Their Faces*."
- "That same year, she became one of four original LIFE magazine photographers and shot the cover for the magazine's inaugural issue (November 23, 1936), featuring the Ft. Peck Dam in Montana."
- "She was America's first accredited female photographer in World War II and the first authorized to fly on a combat mission."
- "Among the first to document Nazi death camps."
- "She was the last person to interview Mahatma Gandhi, meeting with him six hours before his assassination."
- Photography philosophy: "Photography is a very subtle thing. You must let the camera take you by the hand, as it were, and lead you into your subject."
