# 1990s Clervaux installation + Sandeen reception — perspective note

This note accompanies the 1990s bench added in PR #95: 6 Tier-1 institutional
entries (CNA, UNESCO, BnL access barrier) and 8 Tier-2 critical-scholarship
entries (Sandeen reviews in AHR / JAS, Batchen 1997, Squiers 1990, Wells 1996,
Tagg 1992, two Szarkowski interviews). It extends — and should be read
alongside — `research/reception-1970s-critical-theory.md` (PR #91) and
`research/reception-1980s-critical-theory.md` (PR #92).

Three qualifications belong alongside the bench.

## 1. Coexistence, not redemption

The 1992–94 Toulouse / Tokyo / Hiroshima tour and the June 1994 Clervaux
inauguration are documented in this batch through the CNA's and UNESCO's own
institutional voices. Those voices celebrate the restoration, the permanent
installation, and the 2003 UNESCO Memory of the World inscription. That
custodial framing co-exists with — it does not redeem or supersede — the
critical reading developed by Phillips 1982, Sekula 1981 / 1986 / 1995,
Sandeen 1995, Stimson 2006, and Turner 2013: that *The Family of Man*'s
recirculation in Hiroshima specifically, and its reactivation as a "post-Cold
War humanist" object more broadly, is itself a contested act, not a neutral
re-staging. The same plates that closed Section 40 BOMB at MoMA in 1955
returning to the city most associated with nuclear warfare in 1992 is a
curatorial choice that the *October*-axis reading does not treat as
self-evidently redemptive. Both framings should be cited.

## 2. Sandeen 1995 in AHR and JAS — peer reception, not settled reception

The two scholarly reviews added in this batch (`src-ahr-1997-review-sandeen`,
`src-jas-1996-review-sandeen`) confirm Sandeen 1995 was widely received
across American historical and Americanist scholarship. They do **not**
establish Sandeen as the settled reading of *The Family of Man*. The
*October*-axis critique (`src-sekula-1981`, `src-sekula-1986`,
`src-solomon-godeau-1991-photography-at-dock`, `src-phillips-1982-judgment-seat`,
`src-crimp-1993-on-museums-ruins`) and the post-2000 cultural-diplomacy
strand (`src-stimson-2006`, `src-turner-2013`) reach different verdicts on
the same exhibition.

## 3. Anglophone bias persists in this batch

The Bourdieu / Flusser / Eco gap flagged in PR #91 and PR #92 is **not
closed** in this batch. Of 14 entries: 13 are Anglophone or Luxembourg /
UNESCO institutional sources; the only non-English-language voice is the
French-language CNA institutional record. Major non-Anglophone 1990s voices
on photography and exhibition theory remain absent — Pierre Bourdieu's *Un
art moyen* (1965; English 1990); Vilém Flusser, *Für eine Philosophie der
Fotografie* (1983; English 2000); Umberto Eco's photography essays in *Faith
in Fakes / Travels in Hyperreality* (1973–1986); Gisèle Freund,
*Photographie et société* (1974). Future batches should address this.
