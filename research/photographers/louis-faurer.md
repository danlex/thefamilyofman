# Louis Faurer (1916–2001)

Louis Faurer was an American street photographer associated with the New York School, known for his lyrical, emotionally charged images of urban life in Philadelphia and New York. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-19) gives his dates as "1916 - 2001" and his nationality as "American" (src-icp-louis-faurer-archive). The SFMOMA artist page (fetched 2026-05-19) adds city-level resolution: "1916, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania — 2001, New York, New York" (src-sfmoma-louis-faurer-archive). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-19) records the day-month tokens "August 28, 1916" and "March 2, 2001" (src-wikipedia-faurer-pointer). The day-month tokens remain at pointer-only status; the city-level anchors (Philadelphia, New York) are corroborated by two independent Tier-1 institutional sources (ICP + SFMOMA).

## Career

The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-19) describes Faurer as "an American candid street photographer" who "initially worked in fashion photography for major magazines including Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, and Life" (src-wikipedia-faurer-pointer). The NYT obituary of record (src-nyt-2001-faurer-obit, in repo, `verified: false`) carries the same commercial-fashion-to-personal-work arc, noting his "rediscovery" in a 1977 retrospective. The ICP page records that his work is "represented in the Museum of Modern Art's photography collection" (src-icp-louis-faurer-archive).

The Wikipedia pointer adds that Faurer "experimented extensively with technical approaches including blur, grain, double exposures, and reflections. Later in his career, he worked with 16mm film using Arriflex cameras and taught at institutions including Parsons School of Design and Yale University. A serious accident in 1984 ended his photographic practice" (src-wikipedia-faurer-pointer). The ICP archive of his work lists five items — urban subjects from New York and Philadelphia. None of the Tier-1 sources fetched this round (ICP, SFMOMA) carry a career-narrative text; the biographical arc above draws on the Wikipedia pointer and the pre-existing NYT obituary.

The first two plates carry no publication credit; the third carries "LIFE" as a publication credit alongside the "American" nationality string. The Wikipedia pointer carries a specific FoM-connection anecdote: "One of Faurer's 1950 photographs from the San Gennaro Festival featuring Robert Frank and his wife Mary attracted the attention of curator Edward Steichen. Steichen included this work in the Museum of Modern Art's traveling exhibition 'The Family of Man' (1955)" (src-wikipedia-faurer-pointer, fetched 2026-05-19).
