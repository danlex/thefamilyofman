# Werner Bischof (1916–1954)

Werner Bischof was a Swiss photojournalist and an early Magnum Photos full member. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) gives his dates as "1916 - 1954" and nationality as "Swiss" (src-icp-werner-bischof-archive); the Magnum Photos profile (fetched 2026-05-09) renders the same anchors as separate sidebar paragraphs ("b. 1916", "d. 1954", "Swiss") and writes verbatim that he "died in a road accident in the Andes on May 16, 1954" (src-magnum-werner-bischof). The MoMA Master Checklist (in repo) records his nationality as "Swiss" verbatim.

## Career

The Magnum profile (fetched 2026-05-09) records that Bischof "was born in Switzerland in 1916. He studied photography with Hans Finsler in his native Zurich at the School for Arts and Crafts, then opened a photography and advertising studio. In 1942, he became a freelancer for Du magazine, which published his first major photo essays in 1943. Bischof received international recognition after the publication of his 1945 reportage on the devastation caused by the Second World War" (src-magnum-werner-bischof).

The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) frames Bischof's documentary trajectory in slightly different terms: "Werner Bischof's photographs of post-World War II European and Asian cultures were integral to the development of photojournalism since 1945" and notes that "in 1949 he joined Magnum, a co-operative picture agency founded by Henri Cartier-Bresson, Robert Capa, Chim (David Seymour), and George Rodger, with the hope of resolving the conflict between his artistic intentions and the often sensationalist journalism that commercial picture editors preferred" (src-icp-werner-bischof-archive). The ICP page also records that "in 1954 he published Japan, with photographs from a year spent living there" — situating the *Family of Man* Japan plates within the same field campaign.

Both Magnum and ICP date his death to May 1954 in Peru (src-icp-werner-bischof-archive; src-magnum-werner-bischof); the previously-recorded news-of-record date "May 16, 1954" (src-nyt-1954-bischof-obit, in repo, marked `verified: false`) is corroborated by the Magnum page's verbatim "May 16, 1954" rendering. The Magnum page adds that Bischof died "only nine days before Magnum founder Robert Capa lost his life in Indochina" (src-magnum-werner-bischof) — a frame Magnum's text uses to mark the cooperative's catastrophic 1954.

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Bischof is represented by six plates in the MoMA Master Checklist, count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` (2026-05-09): photo-0027 (#30, Marriage, Japan), photo-0192 (#194, Classical Music, Japan), photo-0208 (#217, Adult Play, Hungary), photo-0272 (#283, Ring Around the Rosy, Romania), photo-0381 (#395, Famine, India), photo-0436 (#451, Faces, Indochina). Five of these carry a Magnum agency credit; photo-0436 prints only "Swiss" without Magnum, an idiosyncrasy of the checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). Because Bischof died May 16, 1954 — eight months before *The Family of Man* opened on 24 January 1955 — every Bischof print in the show is posthumous, drawn from material Steichen and Wayne Miller had selected from his Japan, Indian, and Indochina assignments earlier.
