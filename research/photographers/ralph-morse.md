# Ralph Morse (1917–2014)

Ralph Morse was an American photojournalist, staff photographer at *LIFE* magazine for thirty years, best known for his WWII combat coverage and his extensive documentation of NASA's early space program. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-19) gives his dates as "1917 - 2014" and his nationality as "American" (src-icp-ralph-morse-archive). The pre-existing NYT obituary stub (src-nyt-2014-morse-obit, `verified: false`) carries the same 1917 birth year and records December 7, 2014 as the death date. A birth year discrepancy exists: the LIFE archive page (fetched 2026-05-19) gives 1918, while ICP and the NYT stub both give 1917. This repo retains 1917, supported by two sources against one.

## Career

The LIFE archive page (fetched 2026-05-19) provides the following career narrative. Morse began at LIFE in 1939. His WWII assignments included Doolittle's raid on Tokyo, the Guadalcanal landing, and the liberation of Paris. The page quotes the verbatim claim: "the only civilian photographer present when the Germans surrendered to Eisenhower" (src-life-archive-ralph-morse). From 1958 onward, he spent fifteen years documenting NASA's space program. The Mercury Seven astronauts dubbed him the "Eighth Astronaut." LIFE Managing Editor George Hunt is quoted: "If LIFE could afford only one photographer, it would have to be Ralph Morse" (src-life-archive-ralph-morse).

The ICP archive holds seventeen items by Morse, spanning photojournalism, documentary, war photography, portraiture, and subjects including Eisenhower, military personnel, astronauts, and Paris scenes (src-icp-ralph-morse-archive).
