# Eddy van der Elsken (1925–1990)

Eddy ("Ed") van der Elsken was an Amsterdam-born Dutch photographer and filmmaker best known for his street and bohemian-portrait work in postwar Paris and Amsterdam, especially his 1956 photo-novella *Een liefdesgeschiedenis in Saint-Germain-des-Prés* (English: *Love on the Left Bank*). The Jeu de Paume exhibition page for the 2017 retrospective co-organized with the Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam (fetched 2026-05-09) opens its biographical line as "Ed van der Elsken (1925-1990) is a unique figure in Dutch 20th-century documentary cinema and photography" (src-jeudepaume-elsken). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-09) gives the precise resolution as "Ed van der Elsken (10 March 1925 – 28 December 1990) was a Dutch photographer and filmmaker" (src-wikipedia-elsken-pointer); these day-month tokens are independently corroborated by the structured-data record at Wikidata Q1282569 (`+1925-03-10` / `+1990-12-28`, fetched 2026-05-09 to `.scratch/wikidata-elsken.json`).

## Career

The Jeu de Paume page records that "as a photographer, his preferred subject was the street, and in cities like Paris, Amsterdam, Hong Kong or Tokyo, he enjoyed [photographing] proud figures, full of life and vitality" (src-jeudepaume-elsken). The Wikipedia pointer adds that Van der Elsken came to photography through sculpture: "In 1937, wanting to become a sculptor, he learned stone-cutting at Amsterdam's Van Tetterode Steenhouwerij" (src-wikipedia-elsken-pointer). He moved to Paris in 1950, where his Saint-Germain-des-Prés circle and his Australian model-and-muse Vali Myers became the subjects of his 1956 photo-novella. The MoMA Master Checklist credits Van der Elsken's single FoM plate via the Rapho Guillumette agency (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist), the same Paris-based agency through which Robert Doisneau, Edouard Boubat, and Brassaï's prints reached the exhibition.

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Van der Elsken is represented by one plate in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-09): photo-0212 (Section 18 Adult Play, France, Rapho Guillumette / Dutch, 10 x 12 cm). The single most direct curatorial-pathway anchor for how that plate reached Steichen comes via the Wikipedia article (src-wikipedia-elsken-pointer), which records Eva Besnyo's testimony of an evening meeting in Amsterdam at the studio of Paul Huf where "Steichen explained his plans for an exhibition on mankind and looked at photographs … photographers present included Cas Oorthuys, Emmy Andriesse, Carl Blazer, Maria Austria, Ed van der Elsken, Henk Jonker and several others. Besnyo claims that most photographers did not dare bring too many of their photographs. However, photographer Ed van der Elsken (1925-1990), whom she identified as the best of them all, brought practically his entire oeuvre. Steichen spent a large part of the evening looking at Van der Elsken's images, encouraging and advising the young photographer in his work." This anecdote is reported in Wikipedia as Besnyo's recollection and has not been corroborated this round against the Eva Besnyo (1910–2003) primary archive at Maria Austria Instituut (Amsterdam) or against a Tier-2 monograph on Besnyo; carried as the best Tier-3 curatorial-pathway anchor available in this round. Wikipedia further identifies the plate as "a chiaroscuro, tight frame cropping fragments of the faces of two women and a man who eyes others at an opening or event over his cigarette, and a male hand (possibly v. d. Elsken's) thrusting a wine glass into the foreground" (src-wikipedia-elsken-pointer); this plate-level identification is consistent with the FoM "Adult Play" section but should NOT be promoted to a print-record claim without a fetched MoMA installation print or original-print attribution.
