# Carl Mydans (1907–2004)

Carl Mydans was an American photojournalist who worked briefly for the Farm Security Administration before becoming one of the early staff photographers at *Life* magazine, where he remained until the magazine's 1972 closure. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) records his dates as "1906 - 2004" and his nationality as "American" (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive). The Wikipedia article (fetched 2026-05-09) gives the day-month resolution as "May 20, 1907 – August 16, 2004", with birthplace Medford, Massachusetts and place of death Larchmont, New York (src-wikipedia-carl-mydans-pointer). **Date discrepancy:** the two sources disagree on the year of birth (ICP: 1906; Wikipedia: 1907). This repo records 1907 in `data/photographers.csv` per the Wikipedia opening sentence and infobox; the ICP discrepancy is flagged in the source-file notes for promotion against a Tier-1 obituary in a future round.

## Career

The ICP page records: "Born and raised in Boston, he earned a B.S. degree from the Boston University school of Journalism in 1930" (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive). Per the same page, "Mydans's career as a journalist began with freelance work for the *Boston Globe* and the *Boston Post*. He continued as a staff writer for the *American Banker*, a New York Wall Street daily." In 1931 he "acquired a 35mm camera and entered the world of photojournalism".

The FSA period is dated to 1935 by both ICP and Wikipedia: "He obtained his first important photographic assignment in 1935 after he had joined what was to become the Farm Secruity [sic] Administration (FSA). He began work as a photographer under the leadership of Roy Stryker, who assigned Mydans to document the cotton industry in the South" (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive). The ICP narrative is brief on this period — "Mydans's stay with the FSA was brief" — and describes his shift the following year: "In 1936 he was hired as one of the four photographers to help launch Life magazine; he remained an active staff photographer until the magazine closed in 1972" (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive). Note that the Wikipedia article describes him as "one of its earliest staff photographers" rather than one of the original four, and names the canonical original four as "Alfred Eisenstaedt, Margaret Bourke-White, Thomas McAvoy and Peter Stackpole" (src-wikipedia-carl-mydans-pointer) — a roster that ICP's Eisenstaedt and Bourke-White pages already corroborate (src-icp-eisenstaedt-archive, src-icp-bourke-white-archive). The "one of the four" framing on the Mydans ICP page is therefore loose phrasing rather than placing him in the canonical first-four roster.

The ICP page records his WWII trajectory: "During World War II, Mydans photographed England preparing for attack, Italy under Mussolini, the Finnish campaign against Russia, Belgian refugees streaming into France, and France at war in 1940 and again in 1944". With his wife and *Life* researcher Shelley Mydans, he was "captured by the Japanese and imprisoned for 21 months" (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive) in the Philippines and Shanghai. The ICP source ends at "imprisoned for 21 months" with no release detail; the Wikipedia pointer records the release as "released as part of a prisoner-of-war exchange in December 1943" (src-wikipedia-carl-mydans-pointer) — pointer-tier carry pending a Tier-1 archival source. After the war Mydans was "chief of the Time-Life news bureau in Tokyo for four years; he went on to cover the war in Korea, then was stationed for several years in England, and for a year and a half in Moscow." He received the US Camera Gold Achievement Award in 1951 (src-icp-carl-mydans-archive).
