# Ihei Kimura (1901–1974)

Ihei Kimura (Japanese: 木村 伊兵衛, *Kimura Ihei*) was a Tokyo-born Japanese photographer best known for his Leica street and portrait work in pre- and post-war Tokyo and his postwar documentary series on Akita Prefecture. The Wikipedia pointer (Tier 3, fetched 2026-05-10) opens its lead as "Ihei Kimura (12 December 1901 – 31 May 1974) was a Japanese photographer, known for his portrayal of Tokyo and Akita Prefecture" (src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer); Wikidata Q3106591 corroborates these structured-date tokens (`+1901-12-12` / `+1974-05-31`, fetched 2026-05-10 to `.scratch/wikidata-kimura.json`), but Wikidata and Wikipedia share the same community-editor pool and are not strictly independent. Day-level resolution remains pointer-only at Tier 3. Year-level dates (1901 / 1974) are also recorded in the in-repo MoMA Master Checklist plate-note for photo-0130 (a downstream catalog-builder annotation, not the 1955 catalog itself).

## Career

Per the Wikipedia pointer (verbatim, fetched 2026-05-10): "Born on 12 December 1901 in Shitaya-ku (now Taitō-ku), Tokyo, Kimura started taking photographs when very young but his interest intensified when he was around 20 and living in Tainan, Taiwan, where he was working for a sugar wholesaler. He opened a photographic studio in Nippori, Tokyo in 1924. In 1930, he joined the advertising section of the soap and cosmetics company Kaō, concentrating on informal photographs made with his Leica camera" (src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer). The 1932 launch of the coterie magazine *Kōga* (光画) with Kimura as a central figure is identified by Wikipedia as having "helped spur the development of Shinkō shashin ('New Photography') in Japan" (src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer); in 1933 Kimura "joined Yōnosuke Natori and others in forming the group Nippon Kōbō ('Japan workshop'), which emphasized 'realism' in photography using 35mm cameras; but this rapidly broke up and Kimura formed an alternative group, Chūō Kōbō ('central workshop') with Nobuo Ina and others" (src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer).

Kimura's mid-1950s European trips supplied photographs for Japanese camera magazines and led to the posthumously-published color collection *Pari* (Tokyo: Norasha, 1974). He died at his home in Nippori on 31 May 1974, and "the Kimura Ihei Award for new photographers was promptly set up in his honor" (src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Kimura is represented by one plate in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-10): photo-0130 (Section 14 *Land*, "Ihei Kimura, Japan, 20 x 14 cm"). The Wikipedia pointer (verbatim, src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer) explicitly attests the inclusion: "In the mid-fifties, Kimura made several trips to Europe, providing photographs for the camera magazines. His work was included by Edward Steichen in the world-touring 1955 MoMA exhibition *The Family of Man*."

The Master Checklist (`src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist`, in repo) credits Kimura's plate with no agency / publication, just "Ihei Kimura, Japan, 20 x 14 cm" — meaning the print reached MoMA directly from the photographer rather than via an agency. The Master Checklist's "Japan" credit is a place-name (where the photograph was made) rather than a nationality designation; this is the convention the checklist uses for some Section 14 *Land* entries.

The Tokyo tour of 1956–57 is the biographically-richest FoM episode for Kimura. Per `src-takenaka-2020-popular-inquiry-japan` p. 46 (Tier 2, in repo, verbatim): "According to the exhibition catalogue of *The Family of Man* in Japan, the Japan tour was promoted by MoMA, USIS and *Nihon Keizai Shimbun*, a major financial newspaper. In addition to the executive chairman, Edward Steichen, the Japanese executive committee consisted of prominent photographers Ihee Kimura, Yoshio Watanabe, Shigene Kanemaru, Yasuhiro Ishimoto, the graphic designer Takashi Kōno who designed the catalogue, and the architect Kenzo Tange who designed the installation, et al." Kimura's role on the Tokyo executive committee is independently confirmed at Tier 2 by `src-takenaka-2022-atomic-bombings` (Bloomsbury, 2022, search-result verbatim, in repo). Kimura's romanization in both Takenaka sources is "Ihee Kimura" rather than the more common "Ihei Kimura"; the two are accepted variant Hepburn transliterations of 木村 伊兵衛 (and the Asahi Camera 1974 obituary number used "Ihee" — see `src-wikipedia-kimura-pointer` reference list).

The Tokyo tour opened at the Takashimaya Department Store on March 21, 1956, and Kimura — as a committee member alongside Watanabe, Kanemaru, Ishimoto, Tange, and Kōno — was directly responsible for the Japanese reproduction set ("Steichen and the committee members decided to recreate the set of more than 500 negatives in Japan and to leave printing and installation of them to the Japanese committee" — `src-takenaka-2020-popular-inquiry-japan` p. 46). The committee's decision to add 60 works by Japanese photographers, including five Yamahata Nagasaki photographs (subsequently censored by curtain at the Emperor's March 23, 1956 visit), is the central Tokyo-tour controversy and is well-attested in the Takenaka 2020 article (src-takenaka-2020-popular-inquiry-japan) and `src-obrian-2008-nuclear-family-of-man` (Tier 2, in repo).
