# Wynn Bullock (1902–1975)

Wynn Bullock was an American photographer and one of the most prominent figures in the West Coast large-format tradition. Wikipedia (fetched 2026-05-19) records his birth as April 18, 1902, Chicago, Illinois, and his death as November 16, 1975, Monterey, California (aged 73) (src-wikipedia-wynn-bullock-pointer). Britannica (fetched 2026-05-19) independently corroborates these same tokens: "Born April 18, 1902, in Chicago, Illinois; died November 16, 1975, in Monterey, California (aged 73)" (src-britannica-wynn-bullock). Two-source corroboration promotes the April 18 / November 16 day-month tokens above pointer-only status.

## Career

Bullock's early career was musical rather than photographic. Britannica (fetched 2026-05-19) records that he "initially pursued voice training at Columbia University before studying law, then finally committed to photography at the Los Angeles Art Center (1938–1940)" (src-britannica-wynn-bullock). Wikipedia (fetched 2026-05-19) expands this: he performed in Irving Berlin's *Music Box Revue* in the 1920s and discovered photography through the work of Man Ray and László Moholy-Nagy while in Paris (src-wikipedia-wynn-bullock-pointer).

Britannica notes his early solarization work influenced by Moholy-Nagy, and records that after meeting Edward Weston in 1948 Bullock "shifted toward realism, focusing on seascapes, landscapes, and nudes." His mature philosophy held that photographs should serve as "equivalents — visual metaphors for larger ideas, such as the passing of time and the inevitability of death" (src-britannica-wynn-bullock). Wikipedia records that his work appears in over 90 major museum collections worldwide (src-wikipedia-wynn-bullock-pointer).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Bullock is represented by two plates in the MoMA Master Checklist, count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` (2026-05-19): photo-0002 (checklist #2, Section 1 Prologue, USA, 50 × 63 cm) and photo-0005 (checklist #6, Section 1 Prologue, USA, 140 × 177 cm). Both are set in the USA and attributed "American" in the checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist).

Wikipedia (fetched 2026-05-19) states: "In 1955, curator Edward Steichen selected two of Bullock's photographs for the landmark *The Family of Man* exhibition at the Museum of Modern Art. His photograph 'Let There Be Light' was voted most popular at the Corcoran Gallery showing, while 'Child in Forest' became one of the exhibition's most memorable images" (src-wikipedia-wynn-bullock-pointer). The identification of photo-0002 as *Let There Be Light* and photo-0005 as *Child in Forest* is widely reported in secondary sources; the MoMA Master Checklist itself does not record titles for these plates.

A title-date discrepancy exists in fetched sources: Wikipedia gives 1951 for *Child in Forest*, while Britannica gives 1954 for *Child in the Forest* (src-britannica-wynn-bullock). The MoMA checklist records no creation date for either plate. The discrepancy is noted and not resolved here; neither year is carried forward as authoritative.

Bullock's two Prologue plates place him alongside Nat Farbman and Harry Callahan in the exhibition's opening cosmological-to-human sequence, and photo-0005 at 140 × 177 cm is among the largest prints in the Prologue.

The Center for Creative Photography (University of Arizona) holds the Wynn Bullock Archive (src-ccp-bullock-archive). The CCP archive URL returned 404 in a prior session (2026-04-24) and was not re-tested in this round; the archive's existence is established in secondary literature.
