# Robert Frank (1924–2019)

Robert Frank was a Swiss-American photographer best known for his 1958 book *Les Américains* / *The Americans*, "one of the most revolutionary volumes in the history of photography" per the ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-07; src-icp-frank-archive). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-07) gives the day-month resolution as November 9, 1924 in Zürich, Switzerland and September 9, 2019 in Inverness, Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia, Canada (src-wikipedia-frank-pointer). The ICP page predates Frank's death and gives no death date.

## Career

Per the ICP page, "Robert Frank began studying photography in 1941 and spent the next six years working for commercial photography and graphic design studios in Zurich, Geneva, and Basel" (src-icp-frank-archive). He emigrated to the United States in 1947, "where Alexey Brodovitch hired him to make fashion photographs at *Harper's Bazaar*." In the early 1950s, "Between 1950 and 1955 he worked freelance producing photojournalism and advertising photographs for *LIFE*, *Look*, *Charm*, *Vogue*, and others" (src-icp-frank-archive).

The ICP page identifies Walker Evans as Frank's most consequential American advocate: "Walker Evans, who became an important American advocate of Frank's photography. It was Evans who suggested that he apply for the Guggenheim Fellowship" (src-icp-frank-archive). The WebFetch summary 2026-05-07 also names Edward Steichen, Willem de Kooning, and Franz Kline among Frank's American supporters — that list is recorded in the ICP page surrounding paragraph but the specific quoted-string framing here is the WebFetch's paraphrase, not literal verbatim per the source.

The Guggenheim Fellowship funded the road trip that produced *The Americans*. Per the ICP page, the result was Frank's "most famous book, *The Americans*, first published in France as *Les Américains* in 1957" (src-icp-frank-archive). The American edition followed in 1959; "After its publication in America in 1959, he devoted an increasing amount of time to making films" (src-icp-frank-archive). The ICP page notes: "Since 1970, Frank has divided his time between Nova Scotia and New York" (src-icp-frank-archive).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Frank is represented by seven plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-07): photo-0031 (Section 4 Pregnancy, USA), photo-0037 (Pregnancy, USA), photo-0244 (Section 23 Food, USA), photo-0310 (Section 25 Relationships, Spain), photo-0365 (Section 29 Aloneness and Compassion, Peru), photo-0375 (Section 30 Aspirations, Wales), photo-0380 (Section 31 Hard Times, England) — all credited "Swiss" in the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). The geographic distribution (USA, Spain, Peru, Wales, England) reflects Frank's pre-*Americans* freelance assignments and his European reportage of the early 1950s rather than the canonical *Americans* road-trip imagery, which was made 1955–1956 and not yet assembled when *The Family of Man* opened on 24 January 1955.

The Wikipedia article (src-wikipedia-frank-pointer, fetched 2026-05-07) describes the seven plates as covering "subjects from Spain, Peru, Wales, England, and the United States, including 'six laughing women in the window of the White Tower Hamburger Stand on Fourteenth Street, New York City'" — the geography matches the strict-match grep above, though the specific identification of the White Tower image with a particular checklist row is NOT verified in this round.

The MoMA Master Checklist credits all seven Frank plates as "Swiss" — Frank had not yet naturalized as a US citizen in 1955 (the year of FoM's MoMA opening). His later "Swiss-American" canonical nationality (per src-wikipedia-frank-pointer) reflects his post-1955 naturalization; that year is NOT verified against any fetched source this round.
