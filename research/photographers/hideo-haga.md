# Hideo Haga (1921–2022)

Hideo Haga (芳賀 日出男, *Haga Hideo*, family-name first) was a Japanese photographer renowned for his six-decade documentation of traditional Japanese festivals, folk customs, and rural culture. Born in Dalian, Manchuria, educated at Keio University, and active from the late 1940s until his death at age 101, he is among the longest-lived photographers represented in *The Family of Man*.

Birth and death dates are anchored at the day level by the Wikipedia pointer (Tier 3, fetched 2026-05-23): born "10 September 1921" in Dalian, Manchuria; died "12 November 2022" at age 101 (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer). Lead verbatim: "Hideo Haga (芳賀 日出男 *Haga Hideo*, 10 September 1921 – 12 November 2022) was a Japanese photographer, known for his photography of traditional Japanese festivals and folk culture."

Name note: The Wikipedia article title uses "Hideo Haga" (given name first, Western order); the Japanese reading order is "Haga Hideo" (family name first), as shown in the kanji rendering 芳賀 日出男. The MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist) credits the plate simply as "Hideo Haga" with no further elaboration.

## Early life and education

Per the Wikipedia pointer (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer): Haga was born in Dalian, Manchuria — reflecting the historical context of Japanese colonial presence in the region. His father was an engineer with photographic interests, and Haga was encouraged to pursue photography from childhood. He enrolled at Keio University in 1941 and graduated with a literature degree in 1944. During his university years he attended lectures by folklorist Shinobu Orikuchi, "which significantly influenced his career direction" (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer) — foregrounding the ethnographic orientation that would define his photographic practice.

## Career

Per the Wikipedia pointer (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer), Haga served in the Imperial Japanese Navy during World War II, "recruited to create aerial photographs." After the war, he was employed by Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (1946) before leaving to pursue photography full-time.

In 1950 he co-founded the Japan Professional Photographers Society and served as its chairman from 1981 to 1988 (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer, verbatim: "served as chairman for seven years in 1981"). His photographic practice focused on traditional Japanese festivals and folk culture across Japan and other countries.

In 1985 he established Haga Library Co. to market "over 300 thousand stock photos" accumulated across six decades (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer). His archive is documented in over 35 published books.

He contributed to Expo '70 in Osaka as "producer for Festival Plaza" (src-wikipedia-haga-pointer).

**Selected publications (verbatim per src-wikipedia-haga-pointer):**
- *Ta no kami: Nihon no inasaka girei* (Heibonsha, 1959) — first book, on Japanese rice festivals
- *Kamisama-tachi no kisetsu* (1964)
- *Japanese folk festivals illustrated by Hideo Haga* (1970)
- *Folk Customs of Japan — Festivals & Performing Arts* (Creo, 1997)
- *Origichi Shinobu to kodai wo tabiyuku* (Keio University Press, 2009)
- *Traditional daily lives along the Japanese Archipelago* series (7 volumes)

**Selected awards (verbatim per src-wikipedia-haga-pointer):**
- 1988: Silver Merit Medal, Vienna
- 1989: Medal of Honor with Purple Ribbon (Japan)
- 1995: Order of the Rising Sun, 4th class, Gold Rays with Rosette
- 1997: Lifetime Achievement Award, Photographic Society of Japan; Iida City Fujimoto Shihachi Photo Award
- 2000: Grand Prize, Pola Foundation of Japanese Culture
- 2008: Travel Culture Study Award

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Haga is represented by one plate in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-23): photo-0035 (Checklist #38, Section 4 *Pregnancy*, Japan, "Japanese", 12 x 8 cm — src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist, Tier 1). The Master Checklist credits the plate with no agency or publication intermediary, suggesting the print reached MoMA directly from the photographer or via the Japanese executive committee.

The Wikipedia pointer provides additional context on this specific photograph (verbatim, src-wikipedia-haga-pointer): his 1952 photograph, described as "Pregnant Japanese Woman Hurrying on Her Way," was "selected, for its (then) unusual public perspective on pregnancy, by Edward Steichen for MoMA's The Family of Man exhibition which was seen by nine million people as it toured thirty-seven countries." The photograph's 1952 date places it three years before the FoM opening — the MoMA Master Checklist does not record a date for this plate.
