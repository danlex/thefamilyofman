# Alfred Eisenstaedt (1898–1995)

Alfred Eisenstaedt was a German-born American photojournalist, hired in 1936 — alongside Margaret Bourke-White, Peter Stackpole, and Thomas McAvoy — as one of the original four staff photographers of *LIFE* magazine, where he remained "for the next 40 years and was active as a photojournalist into his eighties" (src-icp-eisenstaedt-archive). The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-06) records his birth in 1898 in Dirschau (now Tczew, Poland) and his death in 1995, while the Wikipedia article (pointer-only, fetched 2026-05-06) gives the day-month resolution as December 6, 1898 / August 23, 1995, in Oak Bluffs, Massachusetts (src-wikipedia-eisenstaedt-pointer).

## Career

Eisenstaedt "studied at the University of Berlin and served in the German army during World War I"; he was self-taught in photography while working as a salesman in Berlin and became a freelance photojournalist in the late 1920s (src-icp-eisenstaedt-archive). His career launched in 1929 with his first major assignment — "the Nobel Prize ceremony in Stockholm" — after which he worked for the Pacific and Atlantic Picture Agency (later Associated Press) until 1935. That year he emigrated to the United States, freelancing for *Harper's Bazaar*, *Vogue*, and *Town and Country* before Henry Luce hired him for the new *LIFE* magazine in 1936 (src-icp-eisenstaedt-archive).

Eisenstaedt's V-J Day photograph of an American sailor and nurse kissing in Times Square (1945) is among the most reproduced photographs of the twentieth century. The LIFE archive page (fetched 2026-05-06) preserves his own description of the moment: "I ran ahead of him because I had Leica cameras around my neck, focused from 10 feet to infinity," and he "snapped five times" to capture it (src-life-archive-eisenstaedt-bio). He received the ICP Infinity Master of Photography Award in 1988 per the ICP page (src-icp-eisenstaedt-archive).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Eisenstaedt is represented by seven plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-06): photo-0068 (Section 9 Children A, USA), photo-0105 (Section 12 Fathers and Sons, USA), photo-0284 (Section 25 Relationships, USA), photo-0324 (Learning, USA), photo-0326 (Learning, USA), photo-0327 (Learning, USA), and photo-0443 (later sequence, USA) — all credited "LIFE, American" in the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). His significant LIFE-magazine cluster reflects Steichen's heavy reliance on the LIFE editorial archive in assembling the exhibition's American material; *LIFE* was both a contributor and a publishing partner for the 1955 catalog.

The 1936 four-original-LIFE-photographer roster (Eisenstaedt, Bourke-White, McAvoy, Stackpole) is independently corroborated by the ICP page for Bourke-White (src-icp-bourke-white-archive, fetched 2026-05-06), which states that Bourke-White was "one of the first four photographers hired" with her *Fort Peck Dam* on the first cover.
