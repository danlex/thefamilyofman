# Brassaï (1899–1984)

Brassaï — pseudonym of Gyula Halász — was a Hungarian-French photographer, sculptor, draughtsman, and writer best known for his nocturnal photographs of 1930s Paris. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) gives his dates as "1899 - 1984" and his nationality as "French (b. Transylvania)" (src-icp-brassai-archive). Britannica (fetched 2026-05-09) gives day-month resolution: "Brassaï (born September 9, 1899, Brassó, Transylvania, Austria-Hungary [now Romania]—died July 8, 1984, Eze, near Nice, France) was a Hungarian-born French photographer, poet, draughtsman, and sculptor, known primarily for his dramatic photographs of Paris at night" (src-britannica-brassai). The MoMA Master Checklist (in repo) renders the photographer's name without diacritic — as "Brassai" — and records his nationality verbatim as "French" with a "Rapho Guillumette" agency credit on every plate.

## Career

Britannica (fetched 2026-05-09) and ICP (fetched 2026-05-09) agree on the basic biographical arc. Britannica records: "His pseudonym, Brassaï, is derived from his native city. Brassaï trained as an artist and settled in Paris in 1924. There he worked as a sculptor, painter, and journalist and associated with such artists as Pablo Picasso, Joan Miró, Salvador Dalí, and the writer Henry Miller. ... His pictures were published in a successful book, Paris de nuit (1933; Paris After Dark, also published as Paris by Night), which caused a stir because of its sometimes scandalous subject matter. His next book, Voluptés de Paris (1935; 'Pleasures of Paris'), made him internationally famous" (src-britannica-brassai). The ICP page tracks the same path — studies "at the Academy of Fine Arts in Budapest in 1918 and the Akademische Hochschule in Berlin in 1921 before moving to Paris in 1924" — and adds the meeting with André Kertész in Paris that "encouraged him to try photography" (src-icp-brassai-archive).

Britannica records that "When the German army occupied Paris in 1940, Brassaï escaped southward to the French Riviera, but he returned to Paris to rescue the negatives he had hidden there" (src-britannica-brassai). Per `src-wikipedia-brassai-pointer` (in repo, NOT re-fetched this round) Brassaï became a naturalized French citizen in 1949, after years of statelessness — a pointer-tier datum that the Tier-1 ICP page does not corroborate at the year level.

There are minor discrepancies across the sources fetched in this round. Britannica gives the *Paris de nuit* publication year as 1933; the ICP page renders "1932" (src-icp-brassai-archive); Wikipedia agrees with Britannica at 1933. Britannica gives the place-of-death as "Eze, near Nice, France"; Wikipedia's infobox says "Beaulieu-sur-Mer, France" — both communes lie on the same French Riviera coastline near Nice; the discrepancy is recorded, not adjudicated here. ICP records his nationality as "French (b. Transylvania)"; Britannica as "Hungarian-born French photographer".

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Brassaï is represented by six plates in the MoMA Master Checklist, count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` (2026-05-09): photo-0123 (#129, Land, France, 140 x 112 cm — among the largest plates in the exhibition), photo-0195 (#203, Adult Play, France), photo-0250 (#260, Food, France), photo-0285 (#296, Relationships, France), photo-0356 (#370, Religious Expression, France), photo-0403 (#416, Teens, France). All six are set in France and credited with the Rapho Guillumette agency; "Brassai" is rendered without diacritic in every checklist row (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). Steichen had a sustained curatorial relationship with Brassaï: per Britannica, "The Museum of Modern Art in New York City held a retrospective exhibition of Brassaï's work in 1968" — postdating FoM by thirteen years; per the existing Wikipedia pointer (in repo) Steichen also curated a separate Brassaï exhibition at MoMA in 1956, the year after *The Family of Man* (claim NOT independently re-corroborated in this round at the year-level token).
