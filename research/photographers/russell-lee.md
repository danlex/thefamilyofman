# Russell Lee (1903–1986)

Russell Werner Lee was an American documentary photographer best known for his Farm Security Administration (FSA) work during the Great Depression. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) gives the dates and nationality cleanly in its right-hand panel as "1903 - 1986" and "American" (src-icp-russell-lee-archive). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-09) gives the day-month resolution as July 21, 1903 in Ottawa, Illinois and August 28, 1986 in Austin, Texas (src-wikipedia-russell-lee-pointer).

## Career

Per the Wikipedia article, Lee "was hired for the federally sponsored Farm Security Administration (FSA) photographic documentation project of the Franklin D. Roosevelt administration" in the fall of 1936; he "joined a team assembled under Roy Stryker, along with Dorothea Lange, Arthur Rothstein, and Walker Evans" (src-wikipedia-russell-lee-pointer). The ICP page (whose narrative paragraph is rendered as broken markup but whose right-hand panel is rendered cleanly — see source notes) identifies him as "the FSA's longest employed and most productive photographer", working for the agency "until its closure in 1942"; after the war he photographed coal miners for the Department of the Interior and the oil industry for Standard Oil (src-icp-russell-lee-archive). The same source records that "Following a 1965 retrospective exhibtiion [sic] at the University of Austin, Texas, he took a teaching position at the University, where he remained until his retirement in 1973." Lee's most-recognized series outside the FoM context is the Pie Town, New Mexico Kodachrome work of 1940 (src-wikipedia-russell-lee-pointer).
