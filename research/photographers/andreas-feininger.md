# Andreas Feininger (1906–1999)

Andreas Feininger was a Paris-born American photographer with Bauhaus training, best known for his *LIFE* magazine cityscapes of New York (1943–1962) and his many widely-translated handbooks on photographic technique. The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-09) renders the dates cleanly as "1906 - 1999" in its right-hand panel and identifies him as the "son of the painter Lyonel Feininger" educated "at the Weimar Bauhaus" (src-icp-andreas-feininger-archive). Britannica (fetched 2026-05-09) gives the precise registry resolution as "born December 27, 1906, Paris, France" and "died February 18, 1999, New York, New York, U.S. (aged 92)" (src-britannica-andreas-feininger). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-09) corroborates these tokens and renders his nationality as "American photographer and writer (1906–1999)" (src-wikipedia-andreas-feininger-pointer).

## Career

Per the ICP archive page (verbatim, fetched 2026-05-09), Feininger "was a pioneer both visually and technically. Born in Paris, son of the painter Lyonel Feininger, Andreas was educated in German public schools and at the Weimar Bauhaus. His interest in photography developed while he was studying architecture, and he worked as both architect and photographer in Germany for four years, until political circumstances made it impossible. He moved to Paris, where he worked in Le Corbusier['s] studio." The same source records that "Feininger moved to New York, where he was a freelance photographer for the Black Star Agency and then for the U.S. Office of War Information. After working on a retainer basis, he was a staff photographer at [LIFE]." Feininger's reputation as a writer-teacher rested on a series of handbooks; the ICP page summarises this as "Feininger was renowned as a teacher via his publications that combine practical experience with clarity of presentation" (src-icp-andreas-feininger-archive). Britannica frames him as "an American photographer and writer on photographic technique, noted for his photos of nature and cityscapes" who "studied cabinetmaking and architecture at the Bauhaus, the innovative design school in Weimar, Germany" (src-britannica-andreas-feininger).
