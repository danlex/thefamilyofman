# Henri Cartier-Bresson (1908–2004)

Henri Cartier-Bresson was a French humanist photographer and a co-founder of Magnum Photos in 1947 (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive; src-magnum-photographer-bios). The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-06) gives his dates as 1908–2004 and nationality as French, and the Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-06) gives day-month resolution as 22 August 1908 in Chanteloup-en-Brie, France, and 3 August 2004 in Céreste, France (src-wikipedia-cartier-bresson-pointer).

## Career

Cartier-Bresson "studied literature at Cambridge University (1928–29)" and "began photographing in 1931 and purchased a Leica in 1933" (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive). He joined an ethnographic expedition to Mexico in 1934, studied cinematography with Paul Strand in 1935, and assisted filmmaker Jean Renoir in 1936 and 1939, also creating the Spanish Republican documentary *Return to Life* in 1937 (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive). Drafted into the French army's film and photo unit in 1940, he was taken prisoner that year, escaped after three years of imprisonment, and worked for the French underground; he photographed France's occupation and liberation (1944–1945) (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive).

The Magnum Photos directory (fetched 2026-04-24) records that "in 1947 he co-founded the Magnum agency with Robert Capa, Chim (David Seymour), and George Rodger" — a list that is incomplete versus the standard five-co-founder roster (which also includes William Vandivert and Cartier-Bresson himself); the Magnum page corroborates the founding year and the co-founder relationship (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive; src-magnum-photographer-bios). His first book, *Images à la Sauvette* (1952; English title *The Decisive Moment*), articulated the aesthetic for which he is best known (src-magnum-photographer-bios). He won the Overseas Press Club Award four times, the American Society of Magazine Photographers award (1953), and the Prix de la Société Française de Photographie (1959); he left Magnum in 1966 to focus on drawing (src-icp-cartier-bresson-archive).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Cartier-Bresson is represented by eleven plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-06): photo-0028 (Marriage, France), photo-0122 (Land, Indonesia), photo-0182 (Woman's Work, Bali Indonesia — checklist prints nationality as the place name "France" rather than "French"), photo-0249 (Food, France), photo-0277 (Relationships, USA), photo-0352, photo-0357, photo-0395, photo-0427, photo-0466, and photo-0468. All are credited via Magnum in the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). The Cartier-Bresson cluster is one of the largest single-photographer counts in the exhibition, reflecting Steichen's deep reliance on Magnum-syndicated photojournalism for the show's international scope.
