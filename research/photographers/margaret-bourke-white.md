# Margaret Bourke-White (1904–1971)

Margaret Bourke-White was an American documentary photographer and photojournalist — "one of the first four photographers hired" for *LIFE* magazine in 1936, alongside Alfred Eisenstaedt, Peter Stackpole, and Thomas McAvoy, with "her photograph *Fort Peck Dam* reproduced on the first cover" (src-icp-bourke-white-archive; src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio). The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-06) gives her dates as 1904–1971; the Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-06) records day-month resolution as June 14, 1904, New York City and August 27, 1971, Stamford, Connecticut (src-wikipedia-bourke-white-pointer).

## Career

Bourke-White's first major institutional commission was for *Fortune* magazine — "in 1929, she created the lead story for *Fortune* magazine's first issue" (src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio). The next year she became "the first Western photographer permitted into the Soviet Union" (1930) (src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio). In 1936 she collaborated with the writer Erskine Caldwell — her future husband — on the documentary book *You Have Seen Their Faces*, on Southern rural poverty, and that same year became one of the four founding LIFE staffers; the November 23, 1936 inaugural cover of LIFE was her *Fort Peck Dam* in Montana (src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio).

During the Second World War she became "America's first accredited female photographer in World War II and the first authorized to fly on a combat mission," covering "the German invasion of Moscow in 1941" as "the only Western photographer to witness" it (src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio; src-icp-bourke-white-archive). She was among the first to document Nazi death camps. Her later international assignments included "Gandhi's fight for Indian independence, the unrest in South Africa, and the Korean War" (src-icp-bourke-white-archive). She "was the last person to interview Mahatma Gandhi, meeting with him six hours before his assassination" (src-life-archive-bourke-white-bio). Diagnosed with Parkinson's disease in 1953, she made her final photo essay for *LIFE* — "Megalopolis" — in 1957 (src-icp-bourke-white-archive).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Bourke-White is represented by six plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-06): photo-0152 (Section 15 Work A, USA), photo-0337 (Section 27 Death, Korea — likely from her Korean War coverage), photo-0348 (Section 28 Religious Expression, Czechoslovakia), photo-0350 (Section 28 Religious Expression, Korea), photo-0366 (Section 29 Aloneness and Compassion, India — likely from her Gandhi/Indian-independence assignment), and photo-0426. All are credited "LIFE, American" in the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist).

Her Korea, Czechoslovakia, and India plates draw directly on the wartime and postwar reportage assignments that defined her global career — making her one of the contributors whose individual body of work most consistently aligned with the geographic ambition of Steichen's exhibition.
