# Herbert List (1903–1975)

Herbert List was a German photographer best known for his classicist still-life and figure compositions, his pre-war Greece photographs (1937–1939, published as *Licht über Hellas* in 1953), and his postwar Italian work. The Magnum Photos profile (fetched 2026-05-09) renders the dates and nationality cleanly in its right-hand panel as "b. 1903" / "d. 1975" / "German" / "Hamburg" (src-magnum-herbert-list). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-09) records the day-month tokens "7 October 1903" and "4 April 1975" (src-wikipedia-herbert-list-pointer); these day-tokens carry pointer-only status pending a Tier-1 obituary or estate-archive fetch.

## Career

Per the Magnum profile (verbatim, fetched 2026-05-09), List was "born in 1903 into a prosperous mercantile family in Hamburg, Germany" and his work "combined a love of photography with a fascination for surrealism and classicism." He "began an apprenticeship at a Heidelberg coffee dealer in 1921 while studying literature and art history at Heidelberg University" and "while traveling for the coffee business between 1924–28, the young List began to take photographs, almost without any pretensions to art." A pivot point came in 1930 when "he was introduced to the Rolleiflex camera, which allowed him to make deliberate compositions" — and, in a detail directly linking List to a second photographer in this batch, "though List was completely self-taught, his friendship with Andreas Feininger brought him to the next level. After purchasing the very expensive Rolleiflex, List had Feininger visit from the Bauhaus School to teach him how to use it" (src-magnum-herbert-list). The Magnum page records the 1936 emigration as politically forced: "Leaving Germany in 1936 as the Nazis rose to power, List briefly pursued photography as a profession in London and Paris", followed by the 1937–39 Greek period. The same source records the wartime persecution: "Despite his attempts to evade the war in Athens, List was forced to return to Germany in 1941. Because of his Jewish background, he was forbidden to publish or officially work in Germany, and several of his works stored in Paris have been lost" (src-magnum-herbert-list). The Wikipedia pointer adds that "in 1951, List met Robert Capa, who invited him to work as a contributor to Magnum, but he rarely accepted assignments" — explaining the Magnum-contributor (rather than full member) status (src-wikipedia-herbert-list-pointer).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

List is represented by one plate in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-09): photo-0293 (Section 25 Relationships, Germany, TIME, Inc. / German, 20 x 25 3/4 cm). Wikipedia provides the most precise plate-attribution available in this round (verbatim, src-wikipedia-herbert-list-pointer): "List's 1950 picture of a woman, her black dress spread about her, reclining at a respectable distance from an elderly man reading, with one leg of his trousers rolled above his socks and garter, both enjoying the spring sunshine on the front steps of the Glyptothek in Munich, was selected by curator Edward Steichen for the world-touring Museum of Modern Art exhibition The Family of Man, seen by 9 million visitors." The 1950 Glyptothek photograph identification is plausible at the plate level (photo-0293 is the only Herbert List plate in the FoM checklist; it is set in Germany; Munich's Glyptothek matches the German setting), but the plate-attribution mapping should NOT be promoted to a print-record claim without a fetched MoMA installation print or original-print attribution — the Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist) prints the entry with photographer + agency + nationality + dimensions only, not a title. Carried as the strongest plate-identification candidate in the absence of a definitive Tier-1 print record. The Magnum profile does NOT mention *The Family of Man* (verified by string-search 2026-05-09).
