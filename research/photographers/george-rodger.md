# George Rodger (1908–1995)

George Rodger was a British war correspondent, ethnographic photographer, and a co-founder of Magnum Photos in 1947 (src-icp-rodger-archive; src-magnum-photographer-bios). The ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-06) gives his dates as 1908–1995 and nationality as British; the Magnum directory (fetched 2026-04-24) gives the date of his death as 24 July 1995 in Kent, England (src-magnum-photographer-bios).

## Career

Rodger served in the British Merchant Navy before turning to photography. He worked first for the BBC's *The Listener* magazine and the Black Star Agency, then became a war correspondent for *LIFE* magazine during the Second World War, "traveling approximately 75,000 miles" and documenting "the D-Day assault, and the liberation of the Belsen concentration camp" — he was the first photographer to enter Bergen-Belsen in April 1945 per the Magnum biography (src-icp-rodger-archive; src-magnum-photographer-bios). He won 18 campaign medals for his coverage of military operations in West Africa, Eritrea, Abyssinia, and the Western Desert (src-magnum-photographer-bios).

The trauma of Belsen pushed Rodger away from war reportage and toward ethnographic photography in Africa: he "established himself as the group's correspondent for Africa" and became "one of the first European photographers to produce extensive reportage in Africa during the immediate postwar years" (src-icp-rodger-archive). His photographs of the Kordofan Nuba in central Sudan were published in *National Geographic* in 1951 (src-magnum-photographer-bios). Rodger described his commitment to "straight, unmanipulated photographs" that he called "honest and true" (src-icp-rodger-archive). He attributed his photographic ethics to a stated principle quoted on the Magnum directory: "You must feel an affinity for what you are photographing. You must be part of it, and yet remain sufficiently detached to see it objectively" (src-magnum-photographer-bios).

In 1947 Rodger "joined Henri Cartier-Bresson, Robert Capa, and Chim (David Seymour) in founding the international cooperative photography agency Magnum" — the standard five-co-founder list also includes William Vandivert (src-icp-rodger-archive; src-magnum-photographer-bios).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Rodger is represented by two plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-06): photo-0032 (Section 4 Pregnancy, Kordofan — central Sudan, credited "Magnum, British") and photo-0185 (Section 17 Woman's Work, French Equatorial Africa — the colonial federation later dissolved into Chad, the Central African Republic, the Republic of the Congo, and Gabon, credited "Magnum, LADIES' HOME JOURNAL, British") (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist). Both plates derive from Rodger's postwar African reportage; their inclusion gives Steichen's exhibition two of its substantive sub-Saharan ethnographic anchors — though, as noted in the project's broader source files on the show's Africa coverage (src-newbury-2024-african-looks-at-america, in repo, NOT re-fetched in this round), the exhibition's African content is one of the most-critiqued dimensions of its humanist frame.
