# Ansel Adams (1902–1984)

Ansel Adams was an American landscape photographer, conservationist, and photography educator whose ICP archive page (fetched 2026-05-07) describes him as having a career whose "influence on photography is immeasurable" (src-icp-adams-archive). The Wikipedia pointer (fetched 2026-05-07) gives the full name "Ansel Easton Adams" and the day-month resolution as February 20, 1902 in San Francisco, California and April 22, 1984 in Monterey, California (src-wikipedia-adams-pointer).

## Career

Per the ICP page, "Adams was born in San Francisco, and trained as a musician; his interest in photography was catalyzed when he met Paul Strand in 1930" (src-icp-adams-archive). The decisive institutional turn came two years later: "In 1932, with Imogen Cunningham, Edward Weston, and others, Adams founded the group f/64, which promoted use of large-format view cameras, small lens apertures, and contact printing" (src-icp-adams-archive). He "developed this purist approach into the zone system, a controlled method of determining exposure and development" (src-icp-adams-archive).

Adams's relationship with Yosemite is a central biographical anchor: per the ICP page, "He moved to Yosemite Valley in 1937 and lived there until 1962, teaching annual photography workshops from 1955 through 1984" (src-icp-adams-archive). His role in establishing photography as a curatorial subject at the Museum of Modern Art is also recorded on the ICP page: "Adams assisted Beaumont Newhall and David McAlpin in forming the Department of Photography (the first in the country) at the Museum of Modern Art in 1940" (src-icp-adams-archive). He received Guggenheim grants in 1946, 1948, and 1958 (src-icp-adams-archive).

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Adams is represented by one plate in *The Family of Man* — but it was among the most prominently displayed in the entire exhibition. Per the strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` (2026-05-07), photo-0258 is the sole Adams entry: Section 25 Relationships, dimensions 140 × 168 cm in the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist).

The Wikipedia pointer page records the print's exhibition placement explicitly: "In 1955, Edward Steichen selected Adams's *Mount Williamson* for the world-touring Museum of Modern Art exhibition *The Family of Man*, which was seen by nine million visitors" (src-wikipedia-adams-pointer); and: "At 10 by 12 feet (3.0 by 3.7 m), his was the largest print in the exhibition, presented floor-to-ceiling in a prominent position as the backdrop to the section 'Relationships'" (src-wikipedia-adams-pointer).

Note a dimensional discrepancy: the MoMA Master Checklist records photo-0258 at 140 × 168 cm (≈ 4'7" × 5'6"), not 10 × 12 feet. The Wikipedia "10 by 12 feet" figure may refer to an enlarged installation print made for the wall backdrop, while the checklist may record a smaller print held on file. This discrepancy is flagged for follow-up against the MoMA Steichen Archive's installation photographs and is NOT resolved in this round.

The ICP page does not mention *The Family of Man* explicitly.
