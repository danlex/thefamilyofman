# David "Chim" Seymour (1911–1956)

David Seymour — known professionally as "Chim" — was a Polish-American photojournalist, Magnum Photos co-founder (1947), Magnum president from 1954, and one of the foundational humanist documentarians of the postwar era. The Magnum Photos profile (fetched 2026-05-09) opens its biography "David Szymin was born in 1911 in Warsaw into a family of publishers that produced works in Yiddish and Hebrew. His family moved to Russia at the outbreak of the First World War, returning to Warsaw in 1919" (src-magnum-david-seymour). The November 20, 1911 birth and November 10, 1956 death day-month tokens are independently corroborated by both src-wikipedia-david-seymour-pointer (verbatim: "(born Dawid Szymin; November 20, 1911 – November 10, 1956)") and src-britannica-david-seymour (verbatim: "born November 20, 1911" and "November 10, 1956, near Suez Canal").

## Career

Magnum records the European apprenticeship verbatim: "After studying printing in Leipzig and chemistry and physics at the Sorbonne in the 1930s, Szymin stayed in Paris. David Rappaport, a family friend who owned the pioneering picture agency Rap, lent him a camera. One of Szymin's first stories, about night workers, was influenced by Brassaï's *Paris de Nuit* (1932). Szymin, or 'Chim,' began working as a freelance photographer. From 1934, his picture stories appeared regularly in *Paris-Soir* and *Regards*. Through Maria Eisner and the new Alliance agency, Chim met Henri Cartier-Bresson and Robert Capa" (src-magnum-david-seymour). Britannica's framing matches: "Seymour studied graphic arts in Warsaw and in 1931 went to Paris to study at the Sorbonne, where he became interested in photography" (src-britannica-david-seymour).

The Spanish Civil War work (1936–1938), wartime emigration, and family loss are anchored verbatim in the Magnum profile: "Chim photographed the Spanish Civil War from 1936 to 1938. After it was over, he went to Mexico on an assignment with a group of Spanish Republican émigrés. On the outbreak of the Second World War he moved to New York, where he adopted the name David Seymour. Both his parents were killed by the Nazis. Seymour served in the US Army (1942–45), winning a medal for his work in intelligence" (src-magnum-david-seymour).

The Magnum founding and the UNICEF "Children of War" commission are core anchors. Magnum: "In 1947, along with Cartier-Bresson, Capa, George Rodger and William Vandivert, he founded Magnum Photos. The following year, he was commissioned by UNICEF to photograph Europe's children in need" (src-magnum-david-seymour). Wikipedia's lead summarizes the same arc: "Chim was known for his images from the Spanish Civil War, for co-founding Magnum Photos with Henri Cartier-Bresson, Robert Capa and George Rodger, and for his project 'Children of War' with UNICEF that captured the plight of children in the aftermath of World War II" (src-wikipedia-david-seymour-pointer). His death is recorded verbatim by Magnum: "After Robert Capa's death, he became the new president of Magnum. He held this post until November 10, 1956, when, traveling near the Suez Canal to cover a prisoner exchange, he was killed by Egyptian machine-gun fire" (src-magnum-david-seymour). The El Qantara, Egypt town-level token carried in earlier notes is not corroborated by any source fetched in this round.

## Connection to *The Family of Man*

Seymour is represented by four plates in *The Family of Man* (count verified by strict-match grep against `data/photographs.csv` 2026-05-09): photo-0064 (Section 9 Children A, Austria), photo-0276 (Section 24 Ring Around the Rosy, Italy), photo-0304 (Section 25 Relationships, Germany), and photo-0320 (Section 26 Learning, Italy). All four plates carry the "Magnum + UNESCO" commissioner combination per the MoMA Master Checklist (src-moma-exh-0569-master-checklist) — three of the four are explicitly children-themed (Children A, Ring Around the Rosy, Learning), reflecting the curatorial alignment between Steichen's children-section programming and Seymour's "Children of War" / UNICEF body of work.

The FoM inclusion is named explicitly only on the Wikipedia pointer page: "Edward Steichen included his imagery, including his simple but eloquent picture of the worn hands of an older worker clutching a dip-pen to practice the letter 'a', in MoMA's 1955 world-touring *The Family of Man*, seen by 9 million visitors" (src-wikipedia-david-seymour-pointer). The "worn hands of an older worker clutching a dip-pen to practice the letter 'a'" plate identification is plausibly the Italy "Learning" plate (photo-0320) but this match is not verified at the plate level in this round. Wikipedia's footnote 9 cites the 1955 FoM catalog (Steichen et al.) directly. The Magnum and Britannica pages do NOT name FoM (verified by string-search 2026-05-09).
