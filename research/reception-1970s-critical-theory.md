# 1970s photography-theory reception — perspective note

This note accompanies the bench of 1970s photography-theory entries added in
PR #91 (Sontag NYRB essays, Krauss, Crimp, *October* / *Camera Obscura* /
*Afterimage* / *History of Photography* journal foundings, Berger, Benjamin,
Sekula 1984, Coleman 1979, Malcolm 1980, Solomon-Godeau 1991).

The Anglophone critical-theory turn documented in this bench is one strand
of *Family of Man* reception in the period, not the whole picture. Two
qualifications belong alongside it:

## 1. Coexistence, not supersession

While theoretical critique of FoM's humanism was being institutionalized in
US/UK academic venues during the late 1970s and 1980s, the exhibition's
institutional life continued in parallel. The Luxembourg copy (gifted in
1965) was being installed at Clervaux on the partial-display schedule
beginning in 1974; the world tour residue continued to circulate; the 1955
catalog remained in print and was widely reissued. Steichen-tradition
photography retained popular and institutional life throughout the period
when Sontag, Krauss, Sekula, and Solomon-Godeau were dismantling its
theoretical premises.

Phrasing in the source notes that suggests the critical turn "reshaped"
or "displaced" FoM should be read as describing the academic-discourse
shift, not the exhibition's continued public reception. This perspective
note exists to mark that distinction explicitly.

## 2. Anglophone bias in this bench

Major non-Anglophone theory voices on photography from the same period
are not represented in batch 04:

- **Pierre Bourdieu**, *Un art moyen: essai sur les usages sociaux de la
  photographie* (1965; English translation *Photography: A Middle-brow
  Art*, Polity Press, 1990).
- **Vilém Flusser**, *Für eine Philosophie der Fotografie* (1983; English
  translation *Towards a Philosophy of Photography*, Reaktion, 2000).
- **Umberto Eco's** photography essays (most accessible in *Faith in
  Fakes / Travels in Hyperreality*, 1973–1986).

Their absence reflects the batch's stated Anglophone-critical-theory
scope, not their irrelevance to *Family of Man* reception. Future batches
should address this gap. Bourdieu in particular wrote about photography's
class-cultural position in ways that map onto the popular/institutional
reception of FoM more directly than the *October* axis does.
