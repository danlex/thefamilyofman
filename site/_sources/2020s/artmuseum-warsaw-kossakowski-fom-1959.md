## Relevance

Anchors the **Warsaw 1959 venue** of *The Family of Man* world tour at Tier-1 institutional level — closing the second of the issue #157 Eastern-bloc target venues at primary-archive-record strength. Warsaw is the only Polish stop with a contemporaneously-photographed primary-archive record of the installation in this project; the negatives by Kossakowski (Polish photographer, 1925–2001) document the show *in situ* in the Sale Redutowe (Redutowa Hall) of the Polish National Theatre (Teatr Narodowy) in Warsaw.

## Notes

- **Venue identification anchored**: *Sala Redutowa* (Redutowa Hall) within the *Teatr Narodowy* (National Theatre), Warsaw. The Sala Redutowa is a historical eighteenth-century reception hall in the Pl. Teatralny complex that houses the National Theatre; in the late 1950s it served as a major Warsaw exhibition venue. The Wikipedia tour-table fetched 2026-05-09 in `src-wikipedia-fom-tour-list` carries the more granular date range *"Warsaw, National Theatre, Sept 18 – Oct 21, 1959"*; that row is uncited at the row level, but the institutional-archive Tier-1 venue identification here is consistent with it (autumn 1959, National Theatre / Sala Redutowa).
- **Independent corroboration**: the Polish-language Wikipedia article *Rodzina człowiecza* (`src-pl-wiki-rodzina-czlowiecza`, fetched 2026-05-09) gives a fuller Polish itinerary — Warsaw → Wrocław → Wałbrzych → Jelenia Góra → Kraków (Pałac Sztuki, from 5 March 1960) → Poznań → Dąbrowa Górnicza (10–31 May 1960) — citing a 1960 Polish-published catalogue (Marian M. Mikuta, *Wystawa "Rodzina człowiecza": Dąbrowa Górnicza 10–31 maja 1960: [katalog informacyjny]*, 1960; not consulted directly this round). The Zachęta CBWA institutional history page (`src-zacheta-cbwa-fotografika-fom-pointer`, fetched 2026-05-09) and the Museum of Warsaw director's interview in *1854 Photography* (`src-1854-photography-warsaw-fom-pointer`, fetched 2026-05-09) both attest the Warsaw 1959 stop, the latter at the level of national-museum-director on-record statement.
- **Primary-photographic-record significance**: until this round, the project had no contemporaneous installation-view photograph for any Eastern-bloc venue. Kossakowski's three negatives are exactly that: a Polish photographer documenting the 1959 Warsaw stop *in situ* at the Sala Redutowa. They are accordingly the strongest single attestation in the project to date for any Polish, Yugoslav, or Soviet venue beyond the Sokolniki Park 1959 stop (which is anchored at Tier-2 via O'Brian 2008 and Tier-1 institutional via `src-ane-moscow-1959`).
- **What this source does NOT carry**: per-day attendance; the Polish sponsoring organisation (the metadata names the venue but not the organisers); the Polish-language exhibition title (*Rodzina człowiecza*); per-day visitor counts; the relationship to the broader Polish itinerary (Wrocław, Wałbrzych, etc.). These are recoverable from the Polish Wikipedia and `src-mufo-krakow-fom-prl-lecture-pointer` (PL-language scholarly pointers fetched 2026-05-09).
- **Iron-Curtain framing**: per `src-c2dh-fomleg-lasting-legacy` (re-fetched 2026-05-09 in this round), Poland is one of the *two* explicitly-named Iron-Curtain countries the show reached: *"it would go beyond the Iron Curtain (Poland and the USSR)"*. Warsaw 1959 is therefore the project's first Tier-1 anchor for an Iron-Curtain venue beyond Moscow Sokolniki.
